Runway text · Lastdrop taskbar readout, concept (reviewed)

Every limit is a runway, and the readout asks one thing: does it reach the reset? Each row is a short sentence that reads like the clock beside it: window, verdict, one fact. The verdict is the answer itself (Lasts, Out 15:20, 12 min left). Pace decides it (share used against share of the window gone), so nothing has to be held against the time. Run-out is a clock time you can compare with the clock at a glance; under 30 min it becomes a countdown. The fact is always a labelled word: 58% left when you make it, back 16:30 when you don't. Together they read like a door sign, “Out 15:20 · back 16:30”, with no glyph that could be read as “refreshed”. The rows form a fixed four-column table, so the facts line up. Escalation is coded three ways: badge shape (circle-check, triangle, circle-!), weight (only a failing verdict goes semibold) and, for critical, red text. The all-clear state is as quiet as the clock. The hover card and the drop icon count in the same unit, quota left. The card's pace bar is the runway itself: fill = quota left, tick = time left, and a fill that passes the tick means you make it.

Build notes. Badges are two stacked FontIcons, as in WinUI InfoBar (F136/F139 shape + F13E/F13B/F13C mark). The readout is a 4-column Grid with Auto columns, so its width follows the content and it grows to the left only (tray and clock never move). Numbers use tabular figures, so a countdown ticking from 12 to 11 min does not jitter. Durations and rates are formatted with no-break spaces (1 h 10 min, 8%/h), so the card never wraps inside them. The tray icon is rendered per DPI from the same geometry and is not scaled from 16 px. Contrast (on the taskbar / on its hover fill): secondary text 5.8 / 5.2 light and 9.9 / 8.1 dark; critical text 5.4 / 4.8 light (#B8291B, a text shade of the #C42B1C fill) and 8.4 / 6.9 dark; badge shapes are at least 4.5 against the taskbar, where 3 is the non-text minimum.

8Tray icon · 16 × 16

A drop that drains: the fill is area-true (39 % of the drop’s area, not its height), so “how much is left” is honest even at 16 px. It always shows the tightest window. Colour sits only in the badge, as in the readout, except in critical, which also colours the outline. In-place versions also sit in every taskbar above.
