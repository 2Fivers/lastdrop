Pace bars — Lastdrop taskbar readout

The readout answers “will I make it to the reset?” with one comparison the eye makes before reading a digit: where the fill stops relative to the thin pace tick. The tick sits at the share of the window that has gone by, so a fill that stops short of the tick is under pace and a fill that runs past it is burning faster than time passes. Each bar uses WinUI’s ProgressBar anatomy (1 px track, 3 px indicator, whole pixels). While the answer is yes, bars and text are drawn in the taskbar’s own monochrome text colour, like the clock and the tray glyphs, so the readout stays calm and any colour in it means trouble. The 1 px gaps beside the tick are left unpainted, not painted in a guessed background colour, so they hold on the hover plate, on Mica or acrylic and inside the card. The text has one grammar: “42%” is the share used, “ 14:56” is when the window resets (the hover card puts the same glyph beside the word “Resets”), and “Out 15:20” is when it runs out, shown only when that comes before the reset. Warning and critical use the same words and differ in their severity marks: triangle or filled disc, caution or critical fill, outlined or filled tray tile. The words stay in the text colour, so they keep full contrast on light, dark and hover. Columns have fixed widths, so the readout keeps one width in every state and the tray icons never jump.

  • Bar fill = share used, tick = share of the window elapsed. Fill short of the tick: fine. Fill past the tick: ahead of linear pace (still monochrome; it shows the trend). The bar turns caution or critical, and words replace the numbers, only when the forecast (recent burn rate) runs out before the reset. Values round to whole pixels (40 px bar = 2.5 % per pixel). At 150 % the app draws the track and tick 1 device px wide and the fill round(3 × scale) px tall, and cuts the gaps in device pixels.
  • Labels 5h = 5-hour session, Wk = weekly. With two services the provider goes first (“Claude 5h”). The label column is sized once, when the user pins services, from the widest pinned label, so no label is ever cut off and it never resizes with the data.
  • Time text  14:56 = resets at (today: clock time; later: weekday). ▲ Out 15:20 = runs out at, caution. ● Out 15:02 = runs out at, critical (within the hour or at 90 % and above); the hover card adds the countdown. A time is shown only when it is less than 12 hours away (otherwise the weekday), so 12-hour locales drop AM/PM: “2:56” can only mean the next 2:56, and the 39 px column holds in every locale. The value column never needs to fit “100%”: an exhausted meter always shows the critical words.
  • Which meter wins the tray icon and the one-row form: the one forecast to run out first; if none will, the one with the highest forecast at its reset.
  • Accessibility the readout exposes one UI Automation name per meter, verdict first (“Claude 5-hour session: on track, 42 percent used, … resets at 14:56”); see the aria-label on each readout in this file. In a contrast theme the fills use the theme’s text and highlight colours; the severity glyphs, words and tray-tile shapes carry every state without colour.
  • Stale after 15 min without a good reading. Bars and tick freeze at the last reading. If a window has reset since then, its bar is drawn empty, so no old fill is shown as current.
