Lastdrop taskbar readout · concept “Fuel strip”

Each meter is a 6 px fuel tank. The fill is what you have left, and a 2 px line marks where the fill would sit at an even pace until the reset. Fill above the line: you make it. Fill below the line: you won’t. That one shape comparison answers the question before any number is read, and the tank is snapped to whole pixels so it can never contradict the verdict: an on-track fill always clears its line by at least 2 px, and a fill that is behind pace always leaves a gap under it. Beside each tank are two 12 px Segoe UI Variable Small lines in regular weight, on the clock’s 16 px grid. Read together they form a sentence, “58% left / till 14:56”, so neither the unit nor the reset can be misread. (A ↻ glyph would also read as “refreshed at”, and a bare “Thu” does not say which Thursday.) When the pace fails, line 2 becomes the run-out time (“Out 15:20”, “Out in 12m”) and turns semibold, which is the only semibold in the strip. At the same time the fill takes the WinUI caution or critical colour, the provider glyph gets a tray-style badge (triangle, circle ×, circle /) and calm sibling meters drop to one line. Colour never appears without a shape. On stale data the fill goes hollow and the numbers turn secondary. The tray icon shows the same verdict turned sideways, and the hover card repeats the bars as WinUI progress bars with the pace in words.

Build rules (so the native version stays bug-free)
  1. One comparison drives everything. State, colour, badge and the “Out” time all come from the same pace the line draws (used share against elapsed share). If tank, badge and text drew on different maths they could disagree. A smarter recent-rate forecast can go in the hover card as an extra sentence.
  2. Pixel rule. Round the fill and the line to whole device pixels, then enforce at least 2 px of fill above the line when on track and at least 1 px of empty track under it when behind (tray icon: 1 column). Exact values live in the text.
  3. Snap strokes per DPI. Line = round(2 × scale) device px, track = round(6 × scale). Never let the line anti-alias (at 125 % it is 3 px, not 2.5).
  4. No width jitter while a state holds. Tabular figures; reserve two digits per number, so only “100%” and a state change can alter the width. The strip grows to the left, away from the tray.
  5. Reset text. Today: “till 14:56”. Within 6 days: “till Thu”. Later: “till 17 Oct”. Run-out: “Out 15:20”, or “Out in 12m” when it is under an hour away. Nothing ever needs an ellipsis.
  6. Colours follow the taskbar theme, not the app theme. In high contrast, fill, line and text use WindowText, the tint is dropped, and every state still reads by shape.

7 · Hover card, scenario 1

Windows 11 tooltip construction: 8 px corners, 1 px flyout stroke, flyout shadow, solid acrylic fallback (#F9F9F9 / #2C2C2C). It follows the taskbar theme, as tray flyouts do. Inside a flyout the bars behave like WinUI progress bars: accent when fine, caution or critical when not, with the same pace line as the tank. The card covers every window, the pace in words, the forecast, the data age, and the provider and plan. The key at the bottom teaches the tank once.

8 · Tray icon, 16 × 16 at 100 % (in .ld-icon) and 4 × for inspection

The icon uses the same rules as the strip, turned sideways for digits: the most constrained “% left” in 13 px semibold above a 16 px fuel bar with its pace tick. On track, at least one fill column always shows past the tick. Ahead of pace, the fill stops short of it. Critical becomes a solid tile, so it reads without colour. Stale gets the hollow bar and a dimmed number. “100” would not fit at a legible size, so a fully unused state shows the Lastdrop drop over a full bar.
