Lastdrop taskbar readout — concept: Ring gauges (“race the hand”)

Each meter is a clock face of its own window: 12 o’clock is the reset, the thick coloured arc is what you have used, and the hand is now, the share of the window already gone. The ring is built like a WinUI progress ring, a thick indicator over a 1 px track, so “Will I make it to the reset?” is a question of shape: the hand resting on the thin track = you will make it; the hand cutting through the colour = you are spending faster than time. That reads in greyscale, at 16 px. The centre carries the state without colour: a hand while the reading is live, “!” when the meter is nearly empty, “?” when the reading is stale. The same 16 px ring is the tray icon, so readout and icon are one symbol. The text beside each ring says the one thing you would act on, and every time it shows is a clock time you can hold against the clock next to it: “58% left”, “empty 15:20”, and in the right-hand column the reset (↻ 14:56, or the weekday when it is more than a day away). No bare durations anywhere, so nothing reads as “2 h used or left?”. The two rows sit exactly on the clock’s 16 px line grid. Each column is sized once for its widest format, so the readout keeps one width in every state. Text uses the taskbar’s own colours; amber lives in the ring and only the critical forecast turns red.

5-hour: 78 % used at 45 % of the window. The fill has overtaken the hand, so the hand now cuts through the colour. The row switches from “% left” to the forecast: empty at 15:20, reset at 16:30. Two clock times side by side, so “before the reset” needs no arithmetic. Text stays in the taskbar’s text colour (as WinUI does for caution); the amber lives in the ring.

3 · Critical

5-hour: 94 % used, about 12 minutes of use left at the current rate (empty ≈ 15:21); resets 16:30. The ring closes in red and the hand gives way to “!”; the forecast turns critical red (WinUI’s error-text treatment). It keeps the same grammar as the warning, a clock time, rather than switching to “12 min left”, which next to a reset column reads as “12 min until the reset”, the opposite meaning. The clock beside it already shows “now”, and the text does not tick every minute. The countdown is in the hover card and the toast.

4 · Stale data — last reading 47 min old

Same numbers as 1. With no live reading the rings lose their colour, and the hand gives way to a “?” — Fluent’s own “unknown” mark — while the fills keep the last known values. The reset column gives way to the reason and the age. When the cause is an expired sign-in, line one reads “Sign in” and a click opens it. The readout keeps exactly the width of the live layout.

6 · Tight space — collapsed one-row form

When the taskbar is crowded the readout keeps only the most constrained meter (the one forecast to finish with the least to spare — here the weekly in the normal case) and its headline. The reset and the second meter move to the hover card. Like the full form it has one fixed width, sized for “empty 00:00”, so it never jitters.
