Lastdrop — taskbar readout, final design

Concept A “Pace bars” with two grafts: from C, a countdown (“12 min left”, “6 h left”) instead of a clock time whenever the meter is critical, and for any run-out less than an hour away; from D, the service glyph at the head of every row once rows can come from more than one service. The readout answers “will I make it to the reset?” with one comparison: where the fill stops against the tick. It stays in the taskbar’s own monochrome text colour while the answer is yes; caution or critical colour, a severity glyph and words appear together, and only when the answer is no. Everything here is drawn at 1:1 (100 %) or, in readout-150.png, at 150 % with the bars and icons computed in device pixels by the same reference code the native renderer follows (see the <script> at the end of this file).

The card is the readout’s legend: the tick mark stands in front of “% of time gone”, and the reset glyph in front of the word “Resets”, every time.

Drawn at runtime for the taskbar’s DPI from a hand-tuned layout per size (100, 125, 150, 200 %; 175 % uses the 24 px layout centred, 225 %+ the 32 px one). The number is the most constrained meter’s % used. Shape ladder: bare number over a pace bar (on track), outlined caution tile (warning), filled critical tile (critical), filled tile with an empty drop (empty), grey number over a dashed bar (stale), the Lastdrop drop (starting, no services). Each icon is shown at 1× in device pixels and at 4×.
